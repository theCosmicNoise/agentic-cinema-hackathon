THE LONG ODDS

Written by
A. Roy

FIRST DRAFT (White) - August 12, 2026


FADE IN:

INT. MERIDIAN AUTO BODY - GARAGE BAY - NIGHT

Rain hammers a corrugated roof. Sparks fall from a welding torch.

DESMOND HALE, 40s, grease to the elbows, kills the torch and
lifts his mask. Behind him, a wall calendar from ZENITH MOTORS
hangs crooked, the logo faded.

A radio in the corner plays "Sweet Child O' Mine" through static.

                    DESMOND
          You're three weeks late, Marty.

MARTY OKONKWO, 30s, jittery, stands in the doorway holding a
soaked paper bag.

                    MARTY
          I got most of it.

                    DESMOND
          Most of it isn't it.

Marty sets the bag down. A can of Coca-Cola rolls out and
comes to rest against Desmond's boot.

                    MARTY
          There's a guy. Buys salvage titles,
          doesn't ask questions. Pays in cash.

                    DESMOND
          There's always a guy.

                    MARTY
          This one's real. Calls himself
          Cortland. Runs it out of the old
          Delaney building on 1247 Wabash.

Desmond wipes his hands. Says nothing.

                                                        CUT TO:

INT. DESMOND'S OFFICE - CONTINUOUS

A cramped room. A television bolted high in the corner plays
a scene from THE GODFATHER, sound off.

Desmond drops into a chair. On the desk: a copy of the
CHICAGO TRIBUNE, folded to the business section.

He picks up a landline and dials.

                    DESMOND
                (into phone)
          Yeah. It's Hale. Put me through to
          the claims desk.
                (beat)
          No, the direct line. 312-664-9920.

He waits. On the wall behind him hangs a print of
Edward Hopper's NIGHTHAWKS, the frame cracked.

                    DESMOND (CONT'D)
          Tell Vandermeer at STATE FARM I want
          the file on the Kessler wreck.

                                                        CUT TO:

EXT. WABASH AVENUE - NIGHT

A black sedan idles at the curb. Illinois plate: KJ-4471.

CORTLAND VOSS, 50s, expensive coat, steps out. He checks a
phone, then the building.

                    CORTLAND
                (into phone)
          Send it to voss@zenithmotors.com.
          Tonight.

He pockets the phone and goes inside.

                                                        CUT TO:

INT. DELANEY BUILDING - FREIGHT ELEVATOR - CONTINUOUS

Cortland rides up. Fluorescent light stutters.

He unfolds a printout. The header reads NORTHWESTERN MEMORIAL
HOSPITAL. Below it, a name: DR. ALAN REINHOLT, CARDIOLOGY.

                    CORTLAND
                (to himself)
          Four signatures. That's all this is.

                                                        CUT TO:

INT. DELANEY BUILDING - FOURTH FLOOR - NIGHT

An open floor. Filing boxes. Two men wait: BRICK, huge, and
a YOUNGER MAN at a laptop.

                    YOUNGER MAN
          Site's up. Registered it this morning.
          midwestsalvageauth.com. Looks
          official enough.

                    CORTLAND
          It doesn't need to look official. It
          needs to look boring.

Brick laughs. Cortland doesn't.

                    CORTLAND (CONT'D)
          The Reinholt signature. How close?

                    YOUNGER MAN
          Close.

                    CORTLAND
          Close is what put Kessler in the
          ground.

The Younger Man stops typing.

                                                        CUT TO:

INT. MERIDIAN AUTO BODY - GARAGE BAY - LATER

Desmond stands over an engine block, not working. Marty
watches him.

                    MARTY
          You're gonna do something stupid.

                    DESMOND
          I'm gonna do something slow.

He pulls a cardboard box from under the bench. Inside:
manila folders, decades of them.

                    DESMOND (CONT'D)
          Eleven cars. Same adjuster, same
          doctor, same signature. Six years.

                    MARTY
          Why'd you keep 'em?

                    DESMOND
          Because I knew I'd need them and I
          hoped I wouldn't.

He sets one folder aside. On the tab, in marker: KESSLER.

                                                        FADE OUT.


                          END OF EXCERPT
